## What

`GET /api/app/[store]/exports/[jobId]/[part]` (P8 step 8) joins the derived route list. The harness fills only
`[store]` and `[id]`, so today the route is called with no `jobId`, no `part` and no `t`:

- the attack cases pass (A's handle: the neutral 404 of an unknown handle; B's handle: the same 404 for every id), but
  they never present a **valid** signed link of A, which is the case that matters;
- the role matrix fails for the two granted roles of B (`owner`, `admin`): "reaches the handler with store.export"
  expects a status outside 401 / 403 / 404, and without a ready job of B and its token the handler answers its
  neutral 404.

Add a ready export of A and of B to the fixtures, and pass their ids and tokens:

(The same two-line change of `ids` applies to the "member of A and B" 403 case a few lines below.) `ghostify` must map
`fx.A.exportJob` to a ghost ULID like the other ids, so "B's handle, A's ids" compares A's job with a missing one;
both answer the neutral 404. The route streams `application/zip`, which `callRoute` already reads as `[binary n]`.

Expected after the change: A's handle with A's valid link → the neutral 404 for every attacker (storeGuard refuses the
session first); B's handle with A's job and A's token → the same 404 as a missing job (the signature is A's key, and the
lookup is B-scoped); B's `owner` and `admin` with B's own link → 200; B's other roles → 403 (`store.export` is
owner / admin only); A's rows unchanged (a download writes an audit row in the store whose session downloads: B's).

## Why

§J.2: every new route is attacked the day it is added, and the role matrix proves the refusals are the code's. Until
the merge applies this, `npm run test:tenancy` in the P8 worktree reports the two role-matrix rows above for this
route (owner, admin of B: 404 instead of the body); P8's DB suite proves the same rules at the library level
(`test:storage`, section `export`: F's scope on E's job, E's signature on F, an expired signature, 73 h).
